How our questions are audited

Every DrugBug question goes through the same steps before and after it reaches you. Here is exactly what happens, and what it does and does not mean.

The review process

From draft to your screen

  1. 01

    Written for one study level

    Each question is written for a specific study path and level, such as EMT, Paramedic, RN or medical school, so it matches the work people at that level actually do. AI helps draft and organize questions; doses are checked against the FDA label.

  2. 02

    Checked against editorial rules

    Every question must have one clearly best answer, no trick wording, no real patient information, and nothing that asks for a patient-care decision. A question that breaks a rule is pulled automatically until it is fixed.

  3. 03

    Piloted with evaluators first

    New questions start as pilot questions that only DrugBug evaluators see, marked with a yellow banner. A pilot question goes live only after DrugBug’s own editorial approval. Votes alone never publish a question.

  4. 04

    Graded by evaluators during study

    Evaluators are working clinicians and educators. After answering a question they grade its difficulty from 1 to 5, mark it Good, Bad or Not relevant, choose which levels it is more relevant to, flag poor decoys (wrong answers that are too easy to rule out), and leave suggestions.

  5. 05

    Rated by everyone who studies

    Anyone can rate how hard a question felt, from 1 to 5. Ratings are anonymous.

  6. 06

    Monitored continuously

    Questions graded Bad, marked Not relevant, flagged for poor decoys, or missed by most people are pulled for review. A question almost everyone misses may be unclear or keyed wrong, so it is checked again.

  7. 07

    Open to error reports

    Every question has a report button. Reports are reviewed daily, and corrections reach the app over the air without waiting for an App Store update.

How difficulty is graded

1–2Easy
2–4Normal
4–5Hard

Our target in every 10 questions: about 2 easy, 6 normal and 2 hard. The difficulty you see on a question is the average of everyone's grades.

Common medications come up most

Every medication is sorted by how often people in your field actually meet it. Your study path never changes: nurses do not get prehospital-only questions and EMS does not get nursing-only ones. Within your path, a quiz is weighted like this:

50%+Common: the most-used medications and the ones your field lives on
up to 30%Core: everyday drug types
up to 14%Less common
about 5%Specialist, such as oncology

An ER or ICU nurse sees mostly the medications they handle every shift. Specialist medications still appear, just less often, and a specialist tab (for example an oncology quiz) is all specialist questions. Questions evaluators rate as less relevant to an area also come up less often there.

Relevance keeps each level focused

A paramedic drug question may not belong in an allied health path, and a home health nursing path should not be full of ICU or flight questions. When evaluators mark a question Not relevant, they rate how relevant it is from 1 to 5 and tick the levels it is more relevant to. Less relevant questions come up less often there, and we move or remove questions that do not fit.

Evaluators

Want to be an evaluator?

Evaluators are working clinicians, educators and advanced students who grade questions as they study. Enrollment for new public evaluators is paused right now. If you have questions about an existing evaluator account, or your school or department would like its own evaluators, contact VolareMediaLLC@gmail.com.

What this does not mean: evaluator grading is a quality check by DrugBug. It is not clinical validation, accreditation, continuing education credit or an endorsement by any organization. DrugBug is for study only and is not for patient care or clinical decisions.
