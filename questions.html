How our questions are audited

Every DrugBug question goes through the same steps before and after it reaches you. Here is exactly what happens, and what it does and does not mean.

From draft to your screen

  1. Written for one study level

    Each question is written for a specific study path and level, such as EMT, Paramedic, RN, or medical school, so it matches the work people at that level actually do. We use AI to help draft and organize questions, and doses are checked against the FDA label.

  2. Editorial rules

    Every question is checked against our written rules: one clearly best answer, no trick wording, no real patient information, and nothing that asks you to make a patient-care decision. A question that breaks a rule is pulled automatically until it is fixed.

  3. Pilot questions go to evaluators first

    New questions start as pilot questions. Only DrugBug evaluators see them at first, marked with a yellow banner. A pilot question goes live for everyone only after DrugBug's own editorial approval. Votes alone never publish a question.

  4. Evaluators grade questions as they study

    Evaluators are working clinicians and educators. After answering a question they grade it: difficulty from 1 to 5, Good, Bad, or Not relevant (and to which level), plus written suggestions.

  5. Players rate difficulty too

    Anyone can rate how hard a question felt, from 1 to 5. Ratings are anonymous.

  6. We watch the numbers

    Questions graded Bad, flagged Not relevant, or missed by most people are pulled up for review. A question almost everyone misses may be unclear or keyed wrong, so we check it again.

  7. You can report an error

    Every question has a report button. Fixes reach the app over the air, without waiting for an App Store update.

How difficulty is graded

1–2Easy
2–4Normal
4–5Hard

Our target in every 10 questions: about 2 easy, 6 normal and 2 hard. The difficulty you see on a question is the average of everyone's grades.

Common medications come up most

Every medication is sorted by how often people in your field actually meet it. Your study path never changes: nurses do not get prehospital-only questions and EMS does not get nursing-only ones. Within your path, a quiz is weighted like this:

50%+Common: the most-used medications and the ones your field lives on
up to 30%Core: everyday drug types
up to 14%Less common
about 5%Specialist, such as oncology

An ER or ICU nurse sees mostly the medications they handle every shift. Specialist medications still appear, just less often, and a specialist tab (for example an oncology quiz) is all specialist questions. Questions evaluators rate as less relevant to an area also come up less often there.

"Not relevant" keeps each level focused

A paramedic drug question may not belong in an allied health path, and a home health nursing path should not be full of ICU or flight questions. Evaluators mark a question Not relevant and say which level it does not fit, and we move or remove it.

What this does not mean: evaluator grading is a quality check by DrugBug. It is not clinical validation, accreditation, continuing education credit or an endorsement by any organization. DrugBug is for study only and is not for patient care or clinical decisions.
