DrugBug for Instructors

See exactly where every student stands on their medications

DrugBug gives each of your students a private study app, and gives you a live dashboard: the exact questions your class misses most (and the wrong answer they chose), the medications and question styles they struggle with, and an optional class scoreboard.

Start a free pilotSee the demo dashboard

DrugBug does not ask students for names or emails. Each student enters a class key, and DrugBug displays class results to you under the key label, such as Student 07. Results include scores and activity by medication and topic and may show missed questions and the answers selected. Keep your key-to-student list outside DrugBug.

  • What to reteach firstA plain-language summary of the class's biggest gaps, updated as they study.
  • The questions they missLowest-scoring questions with the right answer, the most-picked wrong answer and the explanation.
  • Every drug, class and question styleMedications, drug classes, question styles, body systems and study modes, weakest first, plus a downloadable report.
  • An optional class scoreboardOff unless you turn it on. When it is on, class members see aliases or approved nicknames and displayed scores. Keep labels and nicknames free of names, emails and student IDs.
  • Pro for every studentThe full app, free to them, for as long as your class runs.

Owner tools

Reteach first

The clearest gaps in your class right now, worked out from every answer.

    Questions your class misses most

    Lowest share correct first. A question needs 3 answers to be listed. "Most picked wrong" shows the wrong answer students chose most often, which is usually the misconception to address.

    Medications they struggle with

    Share correct on each drug's questions, weakest first (at least 5 answers).

    Drug classes

    The same answers grouped by drug class, weakest first.

    Question styles

    How they do by the kind of question asked: assessment, when to hold, side effects, teaching and so on.

    Ways they study

    Practice tests, exam mode, the daily 5 minutes, drills and games. Exam-mode scores are usually the most realistic.

    Body systems

    One question can count toward more than one system (epinephrine is cardiology and shock).

    Studying over time

    Questions answered each week. Hover a week for its accuracy.

    Explore every score — search any drug, topic or category

    Nicknames waiting for your approval

    Nothing shows to classmates until you approve it. DrugBug already blocks bad words and look-alike spellings; you have the final say.

    Students

    Click a student for their scores and the questions they missed. Completion is questions answered against the class goal. Reset frees a key for a lost phone or a new student.

    DrugBug knows these students only as Student 01, Student 02… No names, emails or student IDs are collected. Your printed key sheet is the only link between a key and a person, so keep it with your class records.

    Class scoreboard

    Off: each student sees only their own results, and no one else's name, rank or score. On: students see the top 5 and their own place on their home screen and Dashboard, and the weekly prize bugs start. Turn it on only if your school or agency allows students or staff to see each other's displayed scores. Students appear as their class label (Student 07) or a nickname you approved.

    Ranked by correct answers. Each week the top 3 by correct answers that week win a bug.

    Protocol competition

    Your members race on your agency's protocol questions, as individuals and as teams. Teams are ranked by correct answers per member, so a small crew can beat a big station. Members see the team standings and the top 5 people on the Protocols tab of their class scoreboard.
