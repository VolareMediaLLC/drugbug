Resuscitation content is written from published resuscitation guidelines. DrugBug is not an AHA course, does not provide ACLS or PALS certification, and is not reviewed by AHA.

Not a medical device

Every simulation in DrugBug (such as Megacode, On Scene and clinical scenarios) uses fictional practice patients and is not for real human patients. DrugBug is educational software. It is not intended for clinical decision support, diagnosis or treatment of real patients, and it has not been cleared or approved by the FDA. Calculators and interaction notes are practice tools for learning.

Roles and scope of practice

Each level's medication list and study guide (for example Community Paramedic, surgical or anesthesia technologist, dental hygiene, phlebotomy and EKG technician) is a study list of medications that person may see or support. It is not a scope-of-practice determination; scope differs by state, employer, credentialing body and medical direction.

Safety medication lists

High-alert medication drills are adapted from the ISMP List of High-Alert Medications, and look-alike/sound-alike pairs are informed by the ISMP List of Confused Drug Names and FDA/ISMP tall-man lettering guidance. ISMP lists © Institute for Safe Medication Practices (ISMP), used for education with attribution. DrugBug is not affiliated with ISMP. Consult ismp.org for the current lists.

Flashing and animation

DrugBug plays short animations between sections, and a few of them flash: an ambulance light bar, a cardioversion, an x-ray exposure. They are kept brief and below three flashes per second, which is the published limit for photosensitive seizures, but anyone with photosensitive epilepsy may prefer them off. Settings, Reduce motion turns off every animation and flash in the app, and DrugBug also follows the reduced-motion setting on your phone or computer on its own.

Protocols

Agency and facility protocols shown in DrugBug are used with the written permission of their owners and remain their property. National default scopes follow publicly available scope-of-practice models and may differ from your state or agency.

How content is made

Drug monographs, questions, study guides and scenarios are original writing. Some content was drafted with AI assistance, and AI is used to gather updates from FDA labeling each week. Each drug shows its content review status. A content review checks educational accuracy against standard references at a point in time; it is not a guarantee that the content is complete, current or error-free. Content marked as draft has not yet been reviewed.

Pilot questions, marked in yellow, are new questions shown only to evaluators until they are reviewed. Question difficulty levels are averages of ratings from users and evaluators; they are opinions, not a measure of exam difficulty.

Pronunciation

Spoken pronunciations use your device's built-in voice and may not be perfect.
