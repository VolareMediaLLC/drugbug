What's new in DrugBug

Already live here on the web, and on its way to the iPhone app.

Pick your path, meet your bug

Turn the turntable to choose what you study: Everyday Health, Prehospital, Nursing, Medical School, Imaging or Allied Health. Every level comes with its own bug.

Your bug grows with you

Every bug grows from Rookie to Legend as you learn, with wings, armor and a little shine at each stage.

The Terrarium

Every bug you own floats in its own glass bubble. Tap a bubble to switch bugs, then dress it up from a wardrobe of 146 items that fit every bug.

Meds by System

Medicines grouped by what they work on: airway, breathing, cardiology, shock, neuro, OB and more. Each group has its own quiz and keeps your best score.

Big Calls

Stroke, STEMI, sepsis, trauma and ROSC: what to recognize, the clocks that matter and where the patient should go.

Anatomy you can tap

The Great Bug, a 3D patient you can turn. Tap a body part to learn it, then jump to the medicines that act on it or can harm it.

Your Dashboard

Your scores by body system, drug class, question type and each drug, with your strengths and weak spots, so you know what to study next.

Bug music

Bug jazz, bug house, bugstep, bug rock, banjo bug and bug pop to study to. Free has the Plain Bug Beat and a taste of every genre.

BugDex

All 2,000+ medications in one place, explained in plain language, with drug families compared side by side and illicit and street drug education.

Prehospital

EMT, AEMT, Paramedic and Flight Paramedic scopes, plus Community Paramedic and Tactical Medic (TCCC), blood products and hemodynamics.

Nursing

CNA, LVN/LPN, RN and Flight Nurse, with specialty units from ICU and ER to NICU and L&D.

Medical School

Mechanisms to management, with advanced labs for Step 1-style, Step 2 CK-style and Step 3-style study.

Imaging

X-ray, CT, MRI, nuclear medicine and IR: contrast agents, reactions, eGFR and radiopharmaceuticals.

Allied Health

Mental health, dietitians, respiratory therapists, PT/OT, speech-language pathology, surgical and anesthesia technologists, dental hygiene, phlebotomy and EKG technicians, each with its own scope, study guide and questions.

Questions at your level

Every question carries a one-to-four star difficulty tied to your role, so a CNA is not answering flight questions and a flight nurse is not answering CNA ones. DrugBug tracks the ones you have already done and can serve only the ones you have not seen.

Exam prep

NREMT-style, NCLEX-style, Step-style and Registry-style questions: single answer, select-all and put-in-priority-order questions, Exam mode, weak-spot drills and a test-day countdown with a daily goal.

My List

Save the drugs you are studying and see, in depth, how they interact with each other.

Compounds and probiotics

The interesting compounds in vegetables, fruits and vitamins, on flip cards with a Surprise me button, plus probiotics.

The Wormhole and the Disease Lab

Go down the wormhole on any drug, or step into the Disease Lab on 285 conditions: where it came from and how it works inside you, from the cell to each organ, with the subtypes of each condition broken out.

Study

Questions, flashcards, Brain Games and safety drills, plus Scenarios with Megacode: a live monitor and 24 cases.

3D molecules

See the chemical structure of hundreds of drugs, then tap it to spin a 3D model. Turn the hydrogens on or off and zoom in close.

Grow your bug

Earn sticks for every right answer and build your Terrarium of 90+ bugs, faces and gear.

The bugs are alive

Every bug watches you: their eyes follow your cursor around the page. Each level arrives in its own way, too — the Flight Paramedic and the Flight Nurse lift off under a spinning rotor, and the Combat Paramedic vanishes behind a smoke bomb, with the sound to match.

Meet Professor Sage

Every drug in DrugBug gets explained twice. Once in clinical language, and once by Professor Sage, in words anybody can follow.

He turns up wherever something needs saying plainly: what a lab value actually means, why a drug is given the way it is, and the mistake people usually make with it. If the clinical version has not landed yet, his version usually does — and then the clinical one makes sense.

Common questions

Do I need to install anything to try it?

No. Open it in your browser, pick a route and start. No download, and no account until you want one.

Who is it for?

EMT, AEMT, Paramedic, Flight Paramedic, Community Paramedic, Tactical Medic, CNA, LVN/LPN, RN, Flight Nurse, medical students through Step 3, imaging technologists in X-ray, CT, MRI, nuclear medicine and IR, and allied health: mental health, dietitians, respiratory therapists, PT/OT, speech-language pathology, surgical and anesthesia technologists, dental hygiene, phlebotomy and EKG technicians.

Can I use it for patient care?

No. DrugBug is for education only. Always follow your protocols, medical direction, facility policy and current references.

Where do the doses come from?

FDA labeling, with the label and its date shown on the drug page. Anything outside the label is marked off-label and attributed.

Is there a free version?

Yes, free forever, and it is not a crippled one. Every tool in DrugBug — tests, games, scenarios, Megacode, flashcards, the daily 5-minute DrugBug — works on the free plan. It covers about 40 core medications. What Plus and Pro buy you is more medicine to point those tools at.

Do I need an account?

No, but it is worth having one. Without an account your bug, your sticks and your progress live in that browser only, and clearing your history takes them with it. With a free account they follow you to your phone and back. You can sign in with Apple, with Google, or with an email and password, and you can delete the account and everything in it from Settings.

How do I cancel a plan bought on the website?

Open the plans screen in the app and choose Manage subscription. That opens Stripe’s own billing page, where you can cancel or change your card. Cancelling stops the next renewal and your plan stays active until the end of the period you have already paid for.
